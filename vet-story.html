VET PRICING STORY · ONE SCREEN · FOUR SEPARATE FACTS

The vet pricing story, country by country

Four facts per country, side by side — never combined into a score, because they measure different things from different sources. Price level: how expensive the country is overall (Eurostat PPP, EU-27 = 100). Budget share: how much of household spending goes to vet & pet services (‰). Vet-price gap: how far vet-service prices have outrun the country's own overall inflation since 2020 — the pattern that triggered the UK investigation. Channel: who is legally allowed to sell the medicine. The missing number — the actual markup — exists publicly for one country only: the UK's CMA measured practice prices at 2–5× purchase cost (100–400%). That row wears its badge below.

What each column is — and is not

  • Price level — Eurostat PLI for GDP, whole-economy basket at market exchange rates, latest published year (2024). It is not a vet price: no veterinary PPP category exists anywhere. The outlook toggle swaps this column for the Big Mac now-cast — the same country's burger price vs the euro-area average in the latest Economist edition. One item, not a basket, and a different reference (euro area, not EU-27): use it to see where the 2024 official level has gone stale (Türkiye, Hungary dearer; the official view remains the defensible baseline). The two never appear in the same column at once. Their relationship is validated: r = 0.87–0.97 in every overlapping year since 2011.
  • Budget share — HICP weight of "veterinary and other services for pets" (CP0935), per mille of household consumption. Market size in the wallet, not a price.
  • Vet-price gap — derived: the CP0935 vet-services index divided by the country's own all-items index, both rebased to Jan 2020, minus 1. Positive = vet prices outran the country's general inflation. It is consistent with pricing power, but it is not proof of margin — wages, rents and consolidation move it too. And it does not line up neatly with the channel column: decoupled Sweden sits high, vet-banned Spain sits low, but so do coupled France and Ireland. That non-pattern is itself worth seeing.
  • Channel — the sourced classification from the dispensing-rights map; click a chip for the country's rule and statute. The UK badge is the one measured margin in Europe (CMA final report, Mar 2026: retail 2–5× purchase cost; no medicine price cap imposed).
  • Blanks are findings — Türkiye publishes no vet CPI sub-index (TÜİK series needed), Ireland's stops at Dec 2023 (gap shown to then), the UK left the HICP system in 2020, Kosovo publishes almost nothing, and IS/CY/MT/LU were not compiled in the dispensing map. "—" means not published, never zero.
