NAMIK AKMAN · DATA STORY
The cheapest part of your medicine is the medicine
US · EU · Türkiye · 1982–2026 · public producer prices
A veterinary medicine is an active pharmaceutical ingredient that someone has formulated, filled and licensed. So here is a question worth forty-four years of data: does the price of the medicine follow the price of the ingredient inside it?
SCROLL ↓
01 · United States · 1982–2026

Two lines that were supposed to move together.

The green line is what a kilo of bulk active ingredient costs. The orange line is what the finished pharmaceutical sells for. Both are US producer price indexes, both deflated by CPI, both indexed to June 1982.

For the first few years they behave. Then they stop.

02 · The gap

The ingredient got cheaper. The medicine got dearer.

Across forty-four years, in real terms, the active ingredient fell 44.8% while the finished dose rose 145.1%.

The space between those two lines is the entire subject of this story. It opened from the mid-1980s, widened fastest between 2009 and 2019, and has been closing since 2020.

03 · Which side moves?

Two-thirds of the movement is the ingredient, not the price.

Before calling this a pricing story, it is worth asking which side actually swings. Decompose the variance of the ratio and 65.8% of it comes from the ingredient side.

A manufacturer watching its own selling price is watching the quieter half of its own margin.

How this is measured
Variance decomposition of month-on-month log changes, net of covariance, n = 527 monthly changes. The same method the cattle study used to find that 84% of a "cattle cycle" was actually corn.
04 · An interruption

The number I could not give you.

You want a multiple. "Finished medicine costs 4.9× its ingredient." I can compute it. It is meaningless.

The two indexes sit on different base months — June 1981 and June 1982. Rebase them to a common date and the same "ratio" becomes 2.57, or 2.39, or 1.77, or 1.08, depending entirely on which date you pick.

So nothing here is a level. Every figure is a change between two named dates — the only thing that survives a change of base.

05 · Not one book

The industry runs at two speeds.

Split the finished side into its eight therapeutic classes and the average dissolves. Cardiovascular +130.7% and central nervous system +100.9% roughly doubled in real terms. Respiratory, digestive, skin and vitamins went nowhere or fell.

Between the fast group and the slow one there is a cliff: +51.4% down to +5.1%, with nothing in between.

06 · The antibiotic line

Two true statements pointing opposite ways.

Broad and medium spectrum antibiotics have their own published index. Against its input, it did well: price +12.0% while the ingredient fell 29.3% — a spread of roughly 58%.

Against its peers, it did badly: the whole book gained +29.4% on the identical window, and its own parent class gained 26.5%.

That combination is the signature of harvesting a cheap input, not of holding price. Input deflation is not a durable asset — it stops the day ingredient prices turn.

07 · The turn

Every class has peaked. Not one is at its high.

This is the finding that outlives any single product line. All eight therapeutic classes reached their real-terms peak and turned — most between January 2017 and May 2020. The aggregate peaked in May 2020 and has given back 17.2%.

A plan that assumes real price recovery is arguing against eight series at once.

08 · Türkiye

Your costs reprice instantly. Your prices do not.

Now move the question to a market that imports its ingredients. When the lira moves, 68% of it is in the imported input price within the same calendar month.

How much reaches domestic output prices in that month? 17%.

That gap is not a market fact. It is a process choice — and it is where the margin goes.

09 · What it costs

0.64% of margin, immediately. 0.87% by the following month.

Per one percent of lira depreciation, measured on the margin ratio — domestic price divided by imported input, so Turkish inflation sits on both sides and cancels.

Then the effect fades. But read that carefully: it decays into unmeasurability, not into a proven recovery. By six months the correlation is 0.07, which is indistinguishable from nothing.

The honest claim is that the cost is front-loaded — not that it is repaid. Which is exactly why the hedging horizon is a quarter, not a year.

10 · The part nobody publishes

None of this is veterinary.

No statistical agency anywhere publishes a veterinary-only price index. Every BLS product line that can be checked is labelled for Human Use; Eurostat and TÜİK publish no split at all.

So this is the cost structure a veterinary manufacturer sits inside — not its own price book. I would rather say that plainly than dress an industry average as an animal-health finding.

That gap is the open question, and it is the one worth funding.

The margin came from the ingredient getting cheaper — and that is not a strategy, it is a tailwind.
Three things follow. In the US, price off the ingredient cycle rather than a finished-goods benchmark that moves later and less. In the EU, stop looking for a spread — ingredient and finished prices moved within 1.2 points of each other over fourteen years. In Türkiye, hedge the first quarter and move price revision closer to the currency than to the calendar.

European Union
Eurostat sts_inpp_a, geo EU27_2020, unit I15, annual. C211 basic pharmaceutical products, C212 pharmaceutical preparations. Codes confirmed against the source's own category list. Series ends 2023 — no EU claim is made for 2024–2026.
Türkiye
TÜİK via CBRT EVDS, monthly. TP.TUFE1YI.T60 Yİ-ÜFE NACE 21.2 domestic preparations · TP.UFEYD16 Yurt Dışı ÜFE NACE 21 imported input · TP.TUFE1YI.T59 used only to establish that no separate domestic ingredient stage is measured. Currency: OECD via FRED CCUSMA02TRM618N, monthly average of daily rates.
Deflators
US CPI-U (CPIAUCSL) · euro area HICP (CP0000EZ19M086NEST). The Turkish margin ratio needs none — both sides are nominal lira and inflation cancels in the ratio.
Limits
No veterinary-only price index exists in any of the three markets. These are prices, not volumes, and not net of discount. The US CPI has no October 2025 observation, so that month is dropped from deflated series. Cumulative pass-through beyond six months on the nominal Turkish legs exceeds 100% — trend contamination — and is quoted nowhere.
