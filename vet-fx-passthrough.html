Türkiye · Pharmaceutical producer prices · Monthly · 2010–2026

Your costs reprice instantly. Your prices do not.

The parity study showed the Turkish margin ratio halved because the imported input outran what a domestic maker can charge. It could not say how fast, or how much of a currency move actually lands on the margin. This measures both, on 197 monthly changes — and it is the number that sets a hedging horizon.

Everything here is a month-on-month log change, never a level. USD/TRY, the imported input index and the domestic preparations index all trend steeply in nominal lira, and correlating trends manufactures results. β is the pass-through coefficient — the share of a 1% currency move that shows up in the response. Human and veterinary pharma are measured together; TÜİK publishes no veterinary split.

Leg one: the lira reaches your input cost the same month

This is as clean as a lag profile gets. The relationship is at its strongest at lag zero (r = +0.71) and collapses immediately after — r = +0.31 at one month and essentially nothing beyond. 68% of a lira move is in the input price within the same calendar month. There is no warning period and nothing to watch for: the cost has already changed by the time the month closes.

Leg two: it does not reach your price at all, quickly

The same test, imported input against domestic preparation prices.
Imported input → domestic preparations price, by lag

Two things are true here, and the second one is a limit rather than a finding. First: the same-month link is weak — r = +0.18, β = +0.17. Domestic prices demonstrably do not move with input costs inside the month, which is the whole problem. Second: this profile is too jagged to name a transmission lag. Its highest point is lag 7 (r = 0.311) but lag 2 sits at 0.304 — effectively tied. Anyone quoting "it takes seven months" from this chart would be quoting noise.

What it costs: the margin ratio

Domestic preparations ÷ imported input. Both sides are nominal lira, so Turkish inflation sits in both and cancels — this is the one measure that survives a long horizon.
Cumulative effect of a 1% lira depreciation on the margin ratio
Local projection: for each horizon, the ratio's cumulative change regressed on the currency move at month zero
The damage is immediate and front-loaded. A 1% depreciation takes 0.64% off the margin ratio in the same month and 0.87% by the end of the following month, which is the deepest point. After that the effect decays — and, importantly, it decays into unmeasurability rather than into a provable recovery: by three months r is −0.16 and by six it is −0.07, which is indistinguishable from nothing.

Before you quote any of this

1 · The recovery is not proven, only the damage is. The margin effect is strongly measured at zero and one month (r ≈ −0.5) and fades to |r| < 0.15 by six months. That is consistent with the squeeze being temporary — but it is equally consistent with the effect simply becoming too small to detect against everything else moving. The honest claim is that the cost is front-loaded, not that it is repaid. The 12-month figure turns positive; at r = +0.14 that is not a finding and it is not used.
2 · The nominal legs cannot be read past six months. Cumulative pass-through on the raw import and domestic series exceeds 100% at longer horizons — up to 208% — which is not a real quantity. That is Turkish inflation running through both series and being picked up by the regression, exactly the trend contamination that makes the margin ratio the right measure. Those numbers are computed and stored so the contamination is visible, and quoted nowhere.
3 · This is all pharma, and it is prices. No veterinary split exists in TÜİK's data, and no volume series is involved. A margin ratio is not a margin: it says nothing about what was actually sold, or at what discount.
What would settle it: your own purchase-order dates and price-list revision dates for one year of one product. The gap between them, measured directly, is the number this page can only approximate from national statistics.

So what

Three instructions, each with the thing that would change it.
Hedging · now
Hedge the first quarter, not the year.
The measurable damage lands in months zero and one — 0.64% then 0.87% cumulative per 1% of depreciation — and is undetectable by month six. A rolling three-month cover captures nearly all of the exposure the data can actually see, at a fraction of the cost of hedging a full year.
Would change if: the cumulative effect at six months strengthens past |r| = 0.25 in future data, which would mean the squeeze is persistent rather than front-loaded.
Price cadence · next revision
Move price revision closer to the currency, not the calendar.
Input cost repricing is 68% complete inside the month; domestic price repricing is 17%. That gap is the entire margin story, and it is a process choice, not a market fact. An annual or semi-annual list revision guarantees you absorb every move in between.
Would change if: the same-month input→price coefficient rises above about 0.5, meaning prices already track costs and the cadence is not the binding constraint.
Watching · ongoing
Stop waiting for a warning signal. There isn't one.
The lira→input relationship peaks at lag zero with no meaningful lead at any horizon. Nothing in the published data gives advance notice of an input cost move — by the time it is observable it has already happened. Monitoring effort belongs on response speed, not on forecasting.
Would change if: any lag beyond zero reaches r above roughly 0.4 and clearly beats both lag zero and its own runner-up — the two tests this study applied and neither leg passed.

Deflator
None, deliberately. The margin ratio carries Turkish inflation on both sides, where it cancels.
Method & limits All estimates on month-on-month log changes. β is an OLS slope; r is Pearson. Cumulative effects use local projection — one regression per horizon — because summing lag-by-lag slopes double counts autocorrelated currency moves and produced a 204% pass-through that is not a real number.
Lead tests
A peak away from lag zero is only reported as a lead if it beats both lag zero and its own runner-up lag. Neither leg passed, so no transmission lag is claimed anywhere.
What is missing
A veterinary-only price series for Türkiye (the US publishes one, WPU0634; TÜİK does not). Volumes. Net prices after discount. Actual contract and revision dates.
Administered prices
The domestic preparations index is human-dominated, and Turkish human-drug prices are administered — reference pricing under a state decree with a government-set euro conversion rate, plus SGK reimbursement. The weak input→price leg therefore partly reflects regulation, not only pricing behaviour. Veterinary products are regulated separately (Ministry of Agriculture); whether they sit outside the pricing decree was not verified here. The FX→input leg (the 68%) is an import price and carries none of this machinery.
