Where does a Big Mac cost more than in the euro area?

The timeliness complement to the PPP map: Eurostat's full-basket price levels run ~18 months behind, this one-item index is current to the latest half-year. Each bar: how much the same burger costs versus the euro area, at market exchange rates — orange dearer, blue cheaper. One burger is not a consumption basket; treat this as the fast signal, the PPP map as the rigorous one.

← cheaper · 0 = euro-area price · dearer → outlined = EU/EEA single market — parallel trade legally protected; outside it, EU exhaustion does not apply
Do the two surveys agree? Each dot is a country at the latest year both sources cover: horizontally, Eurostat's full-basket restaurants & hotels price level (re-based to the euro area); vertically, the Big Mac's July-edition parity. Points on the dashed line = perfect agreement. Strong rank agreement justifies reading the burger as the timely extension of the PPP series; the off-line points (Denmark, Switzerland — McDonald's prices far below their restaurant sectors) show exactly where a single item misleads.

Source: The Economist Big Mac index — prices collected by The Economist from McDonald's in each country and converted at the market exchange rate on the survey date; published as open data (github.com/TheEconomist/big-mac-data), semi-annual January & July editions since 2000, fetched via this repo's pipeline. The light-blue outline marks the EU/EEA single market (EU-27 plus Norway and Iceland), where EU-wide exhaustion makes parallel trade between member states lawful; Switzerland and the UK sit outside it, as do all candidate and Middle-East economies shown. The Economist's headline index reports the euro area as a single price; this page additionally uses the member-level source prices published in the same repository, so euro countries appear individually where those exist. Individually priced in the headline index: UK, Switzerland, Norway, Sweden, Denmark, Poland, Czechia, Hungary, Türkiye, Romania, Ukraine, Moldova, and — off the map frame but in the ranking — Israel, Egypt, Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain and Oman (Russia until Jan 2022, then discontinued). Never included in the index: Albania, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Montenegro, Kosovo, North Macedonia, Serbia, Georgia and Iraq — The Economist has not priced them in any edition, so no honest value can be shown. Euro members are shown individually using The Economist's own source prices (collected per country to build the euro-area average): most members from July 2011, Lithuania 2014, Latvia/Slovakia/Slovenia 2018, Croatia 2023, Bulgaria 2026 — in editions before a member's first source price it falls back to the bloc average on the map. Percentages are raw valuations at market exchange rates — no GDP adjustment. A single item cannot represent a consumption basket: use the PPP map for structural comparisons and this page for the current direction of travel.
