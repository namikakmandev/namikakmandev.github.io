WHY COSTS ONLY

Selling prices are noisy. Input prices aren't.

The output side
Published US medicine-price indexes are list-basis. Rebates on branded drugs mean net realisation can sit far below list — and the gap itself moves over time. Any claim built on selling prices inherits that noise.
The input side
Bulk ingredient prices are business-to-business market prices — no rebate machinery, no reimbursement, no administered rates anywhere in the chain. Defensible line by line.
So this deck measures only the input side. The output-side findings exist — they live on the full study pages, with their caveats printed next to them.

METHOD

What exactly is being measured.

Producer price index
The average price at the factory gate — what manufacturers actually receive, as collected by the Bureau of Labor Statistics.
"Ingredient" =
Bulk active-ingredient manufacturing — NAICS 325411, medicinal & botanical. BLS PCU325411325411, monthly, Jun 1982 → Jun 2026, 529 observations.
"Real" =
Deflated by US CPI-U. And because index base periods are arbitrary, only changes between two named dates are quoted — never levels.
Excluded by design
All selling-price series (list-basis — page 3), and all non-US markets. One series, one country, one clean claim.

WHAT'S IN THE INDEX

"The ingredient" is a basket.

Not one molecule — every uncompounded active substance US plants ship to preparation manufacturers, weighted by shipment value:
Antibiotics, bulk
Uncompounded antibiotic substances — named explicitly in the industry definition.
Vitamins
Basic vitamin manufacture — ascorbic acid and peers, uncompounded.
Hormones & endocrine
Endocrine products and adrenal derivatives in bulk.
Alkaloids & botanicals
Isolated plant actives — caffeine, codeine, morphine — and milled botanicals.
One basket, no disease-level split published — we probed for sub-series; they don't exist. Human and veterinary uses are inseparable at this stage.
