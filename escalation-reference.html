Reference points, not a price formula. National indices are averages; no individual supplier's cost structure matches them, and the weights here are yours, not evidence. This is a starting point for a conversation about what changed — not what any specific price should be.
Sources — all Eurostat.
Producer pricessts_inppd_m — domestic market, monthly, unadjusted, 2021=100: C21 pharma manufacturing, C total manufacturing, D energy supply. Electricitynrg_pc_205 — non-household band IC (500–1 999 MWh), excluding recoverable taxes, national currency, semi-annual. The standard mid-size industrial band; national currency on purpose, so the FX factor stays separate. Natural gasnrg_pc_203 — non-household band I3 (10 000–99 999 GJ), same basis. Countries without gas networks are legitimately absent. Consumer pricesprc_hicp_minr — HICP all items, monthly, ECOICOP ver. 2: the series that continues past the 2026 rebase. Labour costslc_lci_r2_q — labour cost index, industry B–E, quarterly, seasonally and calendar adjusted. Exchange ratesert_bil_eur_m — monthly averages. The FX factor is a weighted basket of invoice currencies (EUR, USD, GBP, CHF) against the selected country's currency, combined geometrically the way effective-exchange-rate indices are built; cross rates derived through the euro. Bulgaria's lev is pegged and not published monthly. CoverageEach factor is clamped to its own latest published period and the card says so. Countries not publishing a series say "not published" — including the pharma-specific index in several smaller markets. Türkiye is covered through the same Eurostat datasets (candidate-country reporting, same definitions); its indices reflect a high-inflation economy, which is exactly why the FX factor exists as a separate lever.
