What are you paying for growth? PE, forward PE and PEG, with the assumption shown.

A PE ratio tells you what a company earns today. A forward PE tells you what analysts think it will earn next year. PEG divides that by the growth rate — and there the trouble starts, because "the growth rate" is not one number. Divide by next year's growth and a stock looks expensive; divide by the five-year rate and the same stock looks cheap. Most published PEG tables never say which was used, so the number can't be checked. This one shows the growth rate in its own column and lets you switch the basis, so every PEG here is reproducible arithmetic rather than a claim.

How each number is built. Trailing PE = price ÷ last-12-months EPS. Forward PE = price ÷ consensus EPS for the next fiscal year. PEG = forward PE ÷ expected EPS growth (in percent), using whichever growth basis is selected above. A PEG near 1 is the traditional rule of thumb for "growth roughly justifies the multiple" — it is a rule of thumb, not a valuation model.

Why cells are blank. A company that loses money has no meaningful PE: the ratio is either negative or arithmetically undefined, and printing a number there would be worse than printing nothing. Several names in this list are pre-profit, so they show a trailing PE of — and appear only once consensus expects positive EPS. PEG is additionally blank wherever expected growth is zero or negative, since dividing by it produces a figure that looks precise and means nothing.

Caveats. Forward PE moves on estimate revisions as well as on price, so it can fall without the stock getting cheaper. Consensus is an average of analysts who are often wrong in the same direction at the same time, and it is stale between revisions. Multiples are not comparable across industries — a semiconductor cycle peak and a software subscription base earn very different multiples for good reason. This is a snapshot, overwritten on each refresh, not a time series. Private companies (SpaceX among them) have no market price and therefore no multiples at all. Not investment advice.
