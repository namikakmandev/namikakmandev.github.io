VETERINARY MEDICINES · DISPENSING RIGHTS · 34 COUNTRIES

Who is allowed to sell the animal's medicine?

A legal map, not a metric: which channel may sell veterinary medicines at retail in each country — the vet practice itself, a licensed pharmacy, or (in Spain) neither, for the vet. This is the structural fact behind any question about veterinary distribution margins: where the law decouples prescribing from selling, the vet has no medicine margin at all. Every cell below cites a statute or regulator; where a rule could not be pinned to its legal text, the country says to verify instead of guessing. Nothing here is computed from the price-level study — channel rules and price levels are separate assessments.

Sources: one statute or regulator per country, named in each country's panel and in the table — among them RCVS/VMD and the CMA final report (UK), Code de la santé publique L5143-2 (FR), TÄHAV (DE), Real Decreto 666/2023 (ES), D.Lgs. 218/2023 (IT), Animal Medication Act 387/2014 (FI), Zakon o veterinarstvu (RS), Закон за ветеринарно-медицински препарати (MK) and Zakon o ljekovima 14/26 (ME). Compiled August 2026 in this repo's research note (notes/vet-markup-study.md), country by country, with every claim carrying a named source and open questions flagged rather than filled. Four small markets were not compiled (LU, IS, CY, MT) and are left dark on the map. Kosovo has no separate shape on this basemap — its row lives in the list and table. Classifications group each country's statute by its retail channel; within the pharmacy channel, national variants differ (Bulgaria's vet pharmacies must be staffed by veterinarians; Finland's vets may hand over medicines but only at their own purchase price). This page makes no claim about margin sizes anywhere — the only deep public margin evidence is the UK CMA investigation, summarised in the research note.
