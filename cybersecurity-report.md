## 1. Information Security: The Fundamentals

Information security is the practice of protecting information from unauthorized access, alteration, or destruction. It is organized around three goals — the **CIA triad**:

- **Confidentiality** — only authorized people can see the information (encryption, access controls).
- **Integrity** — the information cannot be tampered with undetected (checksums, audit logs).
- **Availability** — the information is there when legitimate users need it (backups, redundancy).

The most widely used blueprint is the **NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0** (2024), which structures a security program into six functions: **Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover** — written so that small businesses and beginners can apply it, not just large enterprises.

> With 62% of breaches involving a human action, every person who opens email or handles customer data is part of the security perimeter — whether they know it or not.

## 2. The Data Breach Landscape in 2025–2026

A data breach is a confirmed incident in which protected data is accessed or disclosed without authorization.

- The ITRC counted **3,322 data compromises** in 2025 — the highest ever, and **+79% over five years**. About 279 million victim notices went out; lower than 2024 only because 2024 was inflated by a few "mega-breaches." Fewer giant events — but more breaches overall.
- IBM's Cost of a Data Breach Report 2025: the global average fell 9% to **$4.44M** (first decline in five years, thanks to faster AI-assisted detection), while the **US hit a record $10.22M**. Healthcare stayed the most expensive industry at **$7.42M**.
- **Attackers move faster than patching:** vulnerability exploitation became the top initial access vector (31% of breaches) while median time-to-patch rose from 32 to 43 days (Verizon DBIR 2026).
- **AI cuts both ways:** attackers used AI in 16% of breaches (AI-written phishing, deepfakes) and unsanctioned "shadow AI" appeared in 20% — while defenders using security AI contained breaches in 241 days on average, a nine-year best (IBM).

## 3. Social Engineering: Hacking People, Not Computers

Social engineering manipulates people into giving up information or access — no malware required. It exploits trust, urgency, fear and authority.

**Techniques to recognize:**

- **Phishing** — the FBI's most reported cybercrime of 2025 (~192,000 complaints, more than double second place); reported phishing losses tripled to $215.8M.
- **Spear phishing & BEC** — targeted attacks, often on finance staff, to trigger fraudulent wire transfers.
- **Smishing & vishing** — phishing by SMS or phone; mobile social engineering success rose ~40% year over year (Verizon).
- **Pretexting** — a believable invented scenario ("I'm from IT, I need your password").
- **AI-assisted deception** — deepfaked voices and AI-written lures; the FBI logged 22,000+ AI-related complaints in 2025 with nearly $900M in losses.

**Tell-tale signs:** unexpected urgency · requests to bypass normal process · slightly-wrong sender domains · anyone asking for credentials or MFA codes.

## 4. Security Awareness: Building the Human Firewall

If 62% of breaches involve a human action, training humans is a frontline defense, not a formality. CISA's national **Secure Our World** program distills personal cyber hygiene into four steps:

1. **Use strong passwords** — long, unique, stored in a password manager (NIST favors length over forced complexity).
2. **Turn on multifactor authentication** — a stolen password alone is no longer enough.
3. **Recognize and report phishing** — verify unexpected requests through a second channel.
4. **Update your software** — with exploited vulnerabilities now the top breach entry point, patching promptly is personal security too.

What good awareness programs share: short frequent training; simulated phishing with feedback, not punishment; a no-blame reporting culture; and visible leadership participation (the "Govern" function in NIST CSF 2.0).

## 5. Data Protection: A Practical Checklist

The consumer stakes, per the Federal Trade Commission: **$12.5 billion** reported lost to fraud in 2024 (+25%), and per FTC testimony nearly **$16 billion** in 2025, alongside more than a million identity-theft reports a year.

**For organizations:** know your data (inventory it) · minimize what you collect · encrypt at rest and in transit · least-privilege access · patch known-exploited vulnerabilities fast (only 26% were fully remediated in 2025) · govern AI use · prepare an incident response plan before you need it.

**For individuals:** follow CISA's four steps · freeze your credit (free, blocks most new-account identity theft) · change reused passwords · if victimized, use the FTC's IdentityTheft.gov and report to the FBI at ic3.gov.

## Key Takeaways

1. **People are the perimeter.** 62% of breaches involve the human element.
2. **The US pays the highest price.** $10.22M per breach; $20.8B in annual reported losses. Prevention is dramatically cheaper than response.
3. **Social engineering is industrialized — and now AI-assisted.** Second-channel verification defeats most of it.
4. **Basics beat gadgets.** Passwords, MFA, updates, phishing reporting — CISA's four steps prevent a large share of real incidents.
5. **Frameworks make it manageable.** NIST CSF 2.0 gives even a small team a complete, credible roadmap.
