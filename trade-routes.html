4,000 years of trade routes in 90 seconds

From donkey caravans carrying tin to Anatolia to the container lanes of today. Each line appears when the route opens and fades when it dies; what stays behind is a faint trace of everything that came before.

2000BC

Sea and river Caravan, road, rail

The routes, in order of appearance

How to read it: lines are schematic, drawn through the main ports and caravan towns rather than along surveyed tracks, and most dates are conventional round figures (a route rarely opens or closes in a single year). Coastlines are today's. The list is a selection: regional networks such as the Grand Canal, the Tea Horse Road, the Mississippian and Mesoamerican exchange systems and Pacific voyaging are left out for legibility, not importance. Figures quoted: Atlantic slave trade embarkations and arrivals from the SlaveVoyages database; Inca road length from UNESCO; seaborne share of trade from UNCTAD; Hormuz oil share from the US EIA. Coastline: Natural Earth (public domain).
