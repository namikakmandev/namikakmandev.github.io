What moved

The ten most unusual year-on-year moves across the whole collection, found by the data itself after each refresh: the daily and monthly sources every morning, everything on the first of the month. Nothing here is chosen by hand: a move is listed when it is far outside that series' own history of yearly changes, and every line names the dataset it came from.

How the list is made

After every data refresh, scripts/whats_moving.py takes each series in the collection whose latest observation is recent, compares it with the value a year earlier, and expresses the difference as a percentage for a level and as points for something that is already a rate, a share or a balance. It then asks how unusual that change is against the series' own record of yearly changes, and ranks by that. A series that swings by thirty percent every year does not make the list for doing it again; one that never moves and just did, does. At most two entries per dataset and four per subject, so one release cannot fill the page.

The sentences are templates filled from the numbers. Read the source line under each: a "biggest rise in the record" for a young series is a smaller claim than the same words for one that starts in 1921. The full note is in data/_moves.json.
