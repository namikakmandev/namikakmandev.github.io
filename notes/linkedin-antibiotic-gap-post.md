## Post copy (EN)

🧬 Europe cut the antibiotics it gives farm animals by more than half in a decade.

It is one of the fastest public-health turnarounds nobody talks about — and it happened without emptying the barns.

But the average hides something remarkable. Measured the only way that makes countries comparable — milligrams of antimicrobial per kilogram of animal biomass (mg/PCU):

🇳🇴 Norway: ~2
🇮🇸 Iceland: ~5
🇸🇪 Sweden: ~13
🇪🇺 EU-27 average: ~85
…and the highest-selling countries sit several times above that again.

Same continent. Same single market. Same veterinary science. A gap of roughly 100× between the lowest and the highest.

That gap is not biology. It is practice.

The low-use countries didn't find healthier animals — they built systems where antibiotics stopped being the default: vaccination programmes, herd health planning, better husbandry and housing, diagnostics before treatment, and prescribing discipline backed by real monitoring.

Which is the genuinely hopeful part of this chart: every country at the top already has a working example of the alternative sitting inside the same market. This is a transferable-practice problem, not an unsolvable one.

Two honest caveats, because the number deserves them: these are sales data, not treatments — they don't tell you how many animals were treated. And species mix matters; poultry- and pig-heavy countries sit structurally higher regardless of discipline.

For those of you working in animal health across Europe: what moved the needle fastest in your market — regulation, farmer economics, retailer pressure, or veterinary practice itself?

Source: European Medicines Agency, ESVAC (European Surveillance of Veterinary Antimicrobial Consumption), final report.

  shaming — safer and more shareable, and it invites practitioners to comment
  with their own market's experience.

## Integrity notes

- mg/PCU is a ratio with a population-corrected denominator, so cross-country
  comparison is legitimate — unlike an index, whose levels are base-dependent.
- Declared on the artifact: sales ≠ administered dose; species mix drives part
  of the spread; the "~100×" gap is stated as approximate.
- The unit, publisher, report edition and year are printed on the graphic.
