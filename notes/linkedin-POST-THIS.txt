Pharma is about 1% of every big economy. The US 0.87%. Japan 0.66%. Germany 0.67%.
 
Then there's Ireland: 16.7%.
 
📊 I went through 35 years of economic data for 41 countries. That 1% barely moves. Not through the genetics boom, not through the switch to biotech drugs, not through COVID.
 
Only five countries break the pattern: Ireland, Denmark, Switzerland, Slovenia, Belgium. They're all small. That's the whole story. The same industry counts for more when the country around it is smaller.
 
⚖️ Germany's pharma industry adds €26bn to its economy. Denmark's adds €36bn — more, from a country a tenth the size. For Germany that's 0.67%. For Denmark, 9.8%.
 
💶 Here's the part I didn't see coming. For every €100 Ireland earns at home, only €75 stays there. The other €25 goes to owners in other countries. Denmark is the opposite: €103 stays for every €100 earned, because Danish companies earn more abroad than foreigners take out.
 
That's the whole economy, not just medicine. But it's the same story. Ireland's factories belong to someone else. Denmark's big company is Danish.
 
🏭 Ireland is where medicine gets made.
🏦 Denmark is where medicine is owned.
 
⚠️ A big pharma share isn't strength. It's exposure.
 
💬 Has another industry ever taken over a whole country like this? I can think of two. Neither ended well.
