## Version A — the counter-intuitive hook (recommended)

The cheapest part of a medicine is the medicine.

Over the last 44 years, the price of the bulk active pharmaceutical ingredient fell
**44.8% in real terms**. The finished pharmaceutical it goes into rose **145.1%**.

I spent a while trying to break that result. It holds — but three things I found on the
way matter more than the headline:

**1. Two-thirds of the movement is the ingredient, not the price.** Decompose the
variance and 65.8% of it comes from the input side. A manufacturer watching its own
selling price is watching the quieter half of its own margin.

**2. There is no single "pharma price".** Split it into eight therapeutic classes and
the average dissolves: cardiovascular +130.7% in real terms, vitamins −13.8%. Between
the fast group and the slow one there's a cliff — +51.4% down to +5.1%, nothing in
between. Broad-spectrum antibiotics gained 12.0% while the whole book gained 29.4% on
the same window. That's the signature of harvesting a cheap input, not of holding price.

**3. Every class has already peaked.** All eight turned between 2017 and 2020. A plan
that assumes real price recovery is arguing against eight series at once.

And in a market that imports its ingredients, the asymmetry is brutal: when the lira
moves, **68% of it is in the imported input cost within the same calendar month — and
17% reaches domestic selling prices.** That gap isn't a market fact. It's a process
choice.

One caveat I won't bury: **no statistical agency anywhere publishes a veterinary-only
price index.** Every BLS product line that can be checked says "for Human Use". So this
is the cost structure a veterinary manufacturer sits inside — not its own price book.
I'd rather say that plainly than dress an industry average as an animal-health finding.

## Version B — shorter, for a busy feed

68% of a lira move lands in a Turkish pharmaceutical maker's imported input cost within
the same calendar month.

17% reaches what it can charge.

That gap is where the margin goes — and it's a process choice, not a market fact. A 1%
depreciation takes 0.64% off the margin ratio immediately, 0.87% by the following month,
then decays into unmeasurability. Which sets the hedging horizon at a quarter, not a year.

The wider story behind it: over 44 years the active ingredient in a medicine got 44.8%
cheaper in real terms while the finished product got 145.1% dearer — and two-thirds of
the movement in that spread comes from the ingredient side, not from the maker's price.

## Notes on posting

- The strongest single image for the preview is the **68% vs 17%** frame — it needs no
  context and it is the most argued-with number.
- Expect the "isn't that just inflation?" reply. Answer: everything is CPI- or
  HICP-deflated, and the Turkish figure is a ratio where inflation cancels on both sides.
- Expect "so antibiotics are doing fine?" Answer: against the input, yes — against the
  book, sixth of eleven and below the market aggregate.
- Do not claim a transmission lag. The input→price profile is jagged; lag 2 and lag 7 are
  effectively tied, and the study says so.
