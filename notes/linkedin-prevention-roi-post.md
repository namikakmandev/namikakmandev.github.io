## Post copy (EN)

"Prevention pays."

Everyone in animal health says it. I have sat in a lot of meetings where nobody could show the line where it stops being true.

So here it is, and it is one division:

📐 Break-even incidence = programme cost per animal ÷ (efficacy × cost per case)

Below that disease incidence, a preventive protocol costs more than the disease it prevents. Above it, every additional point of incidence is margin.

A worked example — €12 per head, 50% efficacy, €250 per case:

▪️ Break-even incidence: 9.6%
▪️ At 15% incidence: +56% return
▪️ At 5% incidence: −48%. The same protocol, the same product, destroying value.

Two things change once this number is on the table.

First, "what is your incidence?" stops being a clinical question and becomes the commercial one. It is what decides whether the protocol is an investment or a cost — and it is specific to that herd, that flock, that unit. There is no universal answer, which is exactly why a universal claim ("prevention pays") is so easy to make and so rarely tested.

Second — and this is the part my industry avoids — in a genuinely low-incidence herd, prevention does not pay on treatment savings alone. That does not mean don't do it. It means the case has to be made on the things this model deliberately does not price: reduced variance in outcomes, welfare, antimicrobial reduction, buyer and trade requirements, and the option value of not having an outbreak in your worst month.

Those are real arguments. They are just not this one. And a business case that quietly relies on them while claiming ROI is a business case that will not survive a CFO.

I put the model on my site so you can run your own numbers — herd size, incidence, cost per case, programme cost, efficacy. Link in the comments. 👇

If you work on the commercial side of animal health: what do you actually use for cost per case? In my experience that single input moves the answer more than anything else, and it is the one nobody agrees on.

## The model

```
benefit per animal = incidence × efficacy × cost per case
net benefit        = benefit − programme cost
break-even incid.  = programme cost ÷ (efficacy × cost per case)
return             = benefit ÷ programme cost − 1
```

Worked example on the poster: €12/head programme, 50% efficacy, €250/case.
Break-even = 12 ÷ (0.5 × 250) = **9.6%**.
At 15%: benefit = 0.15 × 0.5 × 250 = €18.75 → return = 18.75/12 − 1 = **+56%**.
At 5%: benefit = €6.25 → return = 6.25/12 − 1 = **−48%**.
Chart break-evens at €150/€250/€400 per case: 16.0% / 9.6% / 6.0%.

## Integrity notes

- Pure arithmetic; every figure on the artifact recomputes from the printed
  formula. No external data source is claimed.
- The example values are labelled **illustrative, not evidence** on the artifact
  itself — incidence, efficacy and cost per case must come from the reader's own
  records or published trials.
- Exclusions are declared: discounting, sub-clinical losses, variance/risk
  aversion, compliance drop-off, herd immunity. All are stated to bias *against*
  prevention, so the model is conservative in prevention's disfavour — worth
  saying out loud, since it is the opposite of the usual vendor bias.
- Efficacy input is labelled "in practice, not in the trial" to avoid the
  classic efficacy-vs-effectiveness overstatement.
