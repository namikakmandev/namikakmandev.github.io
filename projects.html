Parity: Cattle — the three-year wave

Measured against corn, a kilo of American beef is worth 6.2 times the feed it was worth at the December 1974 low. Behind that drift sits a cycle that has run four times in 55 years: feed gets expensive, herds are slaughtered, and three years later there isn't enough beef. This traces the meat-to-feed parity for the US, the EU and Türkiye on a common 2016 base, times the lag between feed cost and cattle price, and prices the cycle through a feedlot P&L. Every figure comes from the published series and is cross-checked against outside estimates — including the one place the model is thinner than it looks.

Canine Dermatology Medicines — Poland

No published source measures this market. It is estimated here from public data on one principle: clinical rates can be transferred between markets, prices cannot. Prevalence comes from UK primary-care records; every price comes from Polish retail listings. Two independent methods give €30–80M a year, central $65M. A third — scaling Germany's published figure — returns $119–203M and is rejected, because it requires a simple course of skin medicine to cost $160–228, which retail prices contradict. That test, rather than averaging the outlier in, is the part worth reading.

ProjeFin — Infrastructure Project Finance

How do you price and stress-test a multi-billion-lira infrastructure deal? ProjeFin builds the full project-finance waterfall — a construction phase that capitalises interest during construction (IDC), then an operations phase with revenue ramp, escalation, opex and depreciation-based tax. Debt repays by annuity or sculpts to a target DSCR. Wired to Türkiye’s Orta Vadeli Program (OVP): the interest-rate path and a USD/TRY projection flow through every year, and a USD-equivalent equity return shows how much of the lira IRR is just currency depreciation. The Pricing Game

Can you run pricing for a year? You are the pricing manager of a coffee brand in a four-brand market — a mainstream rival that fights back, a premium player, and a discounter that storms in mid-year. Every month brings an event (cost spikes, price cuts, booms, a shortage) and two decisions: your price and one strategic move, each with real effects on demand, trust and cash. Share is split by a logit choice model with a lag, customers punish gouging, and a coach grades every decision — then your P&L is scored against the autopilot. Can you beat it?
