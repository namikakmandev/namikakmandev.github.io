The ingredient and the medicine

A veterinary medicine is an active pharmaceutical ingredient that someone has formulated, filled and licensed. The farm-side studies on this site ask what a kilo of beef buys in feed. This one asks the same question one link up the chain: what a finished medicine sells for, against the price of the active ingredient inside it — and whether the two have come apart.

What this measures, and what it does not. No statistical agency publishes a veterinary-only producer price index. Every BLS product line that could be checked is labelled for Human Use; Eurostat and TÜİK publish no veterinary split at all. So these are the whole pharmaceutical industry — the cost structure a veterinary manufacturer sits inside, not its own price book. Where a therapeutic class close to a livestock portfolio exists, it is shown separately and labelled as the proxy it is.

The United States: the input got cheaper, the output did not

US: active ingredient vs finished dose, in real terms, 1982–2026
BLS producer price indexes, deflated by CPI-U, both indexed to Jun 1982 = 100
Over 44 years the price of bulk active ingredient fell 44.8% in real terms while the price of the finished preparation rose 145.1%. The gap between the two lines is the entire story of this study. It opened steadily from the mid-1980s, widened fastest between 2009 and 2019, and has been narrowing since 2020 — the finished index has given back about 12% in real terms since its 2020 peak.

The closest thing to a livestock portfolio

Antiparasitics, anti-infectives and biologicals dominate what is sold into livestock. These BLS classes still include human products — they are a proxy, not a veterinary index.

All three markets — and they do not tell the same story

Watch the shape, not the level: each market is on its own base and its own span.
Ingredient-to-medicine, three markets on one window, 2010 = 100
The common window all three markets actually cover — the US record runs to 1982, but the EU and Turkish ones do not
On the same sixteen years, the three markets go three different ways. US +76.2% — the spread kept opening. EU −1.4% — ingredient and preparations moved together, which is no spread at all. Türkiye −53.0% — the imported input ran away from the price a domestic maker can charge. One industry, one period, and the answer reverses depending on where you stand.

Before you quote any of this

Three things that would make a number from this page wrong.
1 · The ratio has no meaningful level — only changes. The two US series sit on different base months entirely: the finished index is based Jun 1981 = 100, the ingredient index Jun 1982 = 100. Divide them and you get 4.89, which means nothing. Rebase both to a common date and the same "ratio" becomes:

finished ÷ ingredient, Jun 20262.572.391.771.084.89
So: nothing on this page is quoted as a level, a multiple or a margin. Every figure is a change between two named dates, which is the only thing that survives a change of base.
2 · Türkiye has no measured domestic ingredient stage. TÜİK's NACE 21 index and its NACE 21.2 sub-index are the same series to within 0.185% on average (maximum 1.26%) across all 258 months. Division 21 is carried entirely by preparations — there is no separately measured Turkish basic-pharmaceutical stage to compare against. That is why the Turkish ingredient side is an import price, and why the Turkish result is partly a currency result.
3 · The EU sample is 14 annual observations. Eurostat's C212 preparations index does not begin until 2010 and is not published beyond 2023. Fourteen annual points cannot support a statement about timing, lead or lag, and nothing here claims one. The EU variance decomposition rests on 13 annual changes and is reported only as a direction.

So what — three markets, three instructions

The US is a volatility problem. The EU is a volume problem. Türkiye is a currency problem.
United States · through 2027
Price off the ingredient cycle, not the finished index.
Two-thirds of the movement in the spread comes from the ingredient side, and the finished index has been falling in real terms since 2020. Build the input cost curve into the price review calendar rather than indexing to a finished-goods benchmark that moves later and less.
Would change if: the ingredient share of ratio variance drops below half over a rolling ten years, or the finished index resumes real growth for eight consecutive quarters.
European Union · through 2027
Defend volume. There is no spread here to find.
Ingredient and finished prices fell within 1.2 percentage points of each other in real terms over fourteen years. A margin plan built on European price realisation has no support in the data; one built on units and mix does.
Would change if: Eurostat resumes the C212 series and the two lines diverge by more than 5 points over three years. Until the series is republished past 2023, treat any EU pricing claim as unevidenced.
Türkiye · now
Hedge the input or localise it — the exposure is FX, not pharma.
The parity halved since 2010 and its worst months are lira dates. With no measured domestic ingredient stage, every unit sold carries an unhedged import cost. The 2022 trough has partly recovered, which makes this the moment to fix terms rather than wait.
Would change if: the parity regains its 2010 level for four consecutive quarters, or TÜİK begins publishing a domestic NACE 21.1 index showing local ingredient supply.

Breaks & limits
No survey redesign is documented across any series shown. The US CPI has no observation for October 2025; that month is dropped from the deflated series. EU coverage ends 2023.
What is missing
A veterinary-only price index, in every one of the three markets. Volumes — these are prices only, so nothing here says how much was sold.
