Canine Dermatology Medicines — Poland

No published source measures this market. Poland has no national data on canine skin-disease prevalence and no country-level dermatology figure. This assessment estimates it from public data: clinical rates taken from the UK, and every cost input taken from Polish medicine prices. All monetary values are estimates.

Measured taken from a published source Estimated calculated in this assessment Data cut-off 6 Aug 2026
Estimated market
$32–86M

What dog owners in Poland spend each year on medicines to treat skin problems in their dogs. Central estimate $65M — roughly 260 million złoty.

Two methods built from Polish prices produce this range. A third method, scaling a published German figure, returns $119–203M. That result is rejected, for a specific and testable reason set out in section 05 — it requires a simple course of skin medicine to cost $160–228, which Polish and German retail prices contradict.

01What this measures

The scope is what dog owners in Poland pay for medicines to treat skin problems in their dogs, over one year.

For context, the same treatment counted with consultation fees and tests comes to $76–162M a year. Medicines are 40–55% of that. Published market reports use a wider definition again — adding flea and tick products, supplements and cats — which comes to $108–367M. A report headline is not comparable to the figure above without checking which of the three it counts.

02How the gaps were filled

Poland has no prevalence study and no dermatology market figure, so some data must come from comparable markets. Two categories of input are treated differently.

Transfer principle

Clinical rates are transferred. Prices are not. How often dogs get skin disease is broadly stable across comparable dog populations. What treatment costs varies with income and local price levels. Every clinical rate here comes from the UK; every price comes from Poland.

The 10% chronic share is an assumption and it is the single most influential input in this method. At 5% chronic the average falls to about 180 zł and the market to about $44M. At 20% it rises to about 430 zł and $105M.

Method 2 — share of total treatment spend

The same skin disease counted with consultation fees and tests comes to $76–162M a year, from two independent routes: the dog population, and the size of the Polish veterinary market. Medicines are 40–55% of a treatment episode — the rest is the vet's time and any tests — which gives $30–89M.

The two methods built from Polish prices overlap at $32–86M, with a central estimate of $65M. They share no inputs: one starts from the dog population and medicine prices, the other from the size of the Polish veterinary market.

Why the German method is rejected

Scaling Germany's published dermatology figure to Poland gives $119–203M — above both methods above. Rather than treat that as an open disagreement, it can be tested directly.

The German figure works out at $260–305 of medicine per treated German dog per year. Allow that 10% of those dogs are chronic cases costing $540 a year in medication, and the arithmetic requires every simple episode to consume $228 of medicine. Allow a generous 20% chronic at $650, and it still requires $162. A medicated shampoo and a short antibiotic course cost a fraction of that in Germany as much as in Poland.

The German figure is therefore overstated for this purpose — most likely because it retains more over-the-counter and preventative product than its stated 45.2% antiparasitic share implies. It is reported here for completeness and excluded from the estimate. Supporting this reading: two established publishers give figures a factor of two apart for the same global market.

06Who dispenses it

Almost all of this market passes through veterinary practices, since the significant products are prescription-only. The National Chamber of Veterinarians maintains a complete public register: approximately 7,600 licensed establishments and 10,000 veterinarians, mostly single-practitioner practices, concentrated in Mazovia (about 1,691 businesses), Greater Poland, Silesia and Lower Silesia. At the central estimate this is roughly $8,600 of dermatology medicine per practice per year — a figure worth testing against any known account, as it is a direct check on the whole estimate.

07Recommendations

Report

Quote $32–86M, central $65M, and say it covers medicines only. The same market counted with consultation fees is $76–162M and on the widest published definition $108–367M. A figure given without its definition cannot be compared with a market report or a competitor estimate.

Verify

Test the per-practice figure against a known account. Roughly $8,600 of dermatology medicine per practice per year is checkable by anyone with Polish distribution. If real accounts run materially above or below it, the estimate should move.

Internal

Use internal sales data before commissioning research. Polish revenue for a dermatology product, plus a share estimate, would confirm or refute this range immediately at no cost — and would settle whether the German figure is as overstated as section 05 concludes.

Priority

If buying data, buy the chronic-case share. Not another market-size report. The proportion of treated dogs on long-term medication moves this estimate from $44M to $105M across a plausible range, more than any other single input.

Caution

Treat published dermatology figures as non-comparable until checked. Section 05 shows one failing a basic plausibility test. Two established publishers differ by a factor of two on the same global market.
