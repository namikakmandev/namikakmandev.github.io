Canine Dermatology Market — Poland

No published source measures this market. Poland has no national data on canine skin-disease prevalence and no country-level dermatology market figure. This assessment estimates the market from public sources, using clinical rates from four reference markets and cost data from Poland. All monetary values are estimates.

01What this measures

The scope

The scope is what dog owners in Poland pay to treat skin problems in their dogs, over one year.

That can be counted three ways. Each is a real number, but they are not the same number. This assessment reports all three so a figure is never quoted against something it does not match.

Way of countingWhat is includedResult
Vet visits + medicines Everything the owner pays when the dog is treated at a practice: the consultation, any tests, and the medicines prescribed €70–150M
Medicines only Just the medicines out of the line above. Nothing for the vet's time or tests €30–80M
The wider market-report definition All of the above, plus flea and tick products, shampoos and supplements bought in a shop or online without seeing a vet. Usually cats as well as dogs €100–340M

Published market reports use the second or third row, not the first. A report headline compared against the first row will be wrong by two to four times. Always check which of these three a quoted figure refers to before using it.

02How the gaps were filled

Poland has no domestic prevalence study and no dermatology market figure. Data must therefore be taken from comparable markets. Two categories of input are treated differently.

Transfer principle

Clinical rates are transferred. Costs are not. Disease prevalence, incidence and chronicity are broadly stable across comparable dog populations. Prices, spend per animal and treatment uptake vary with income, veterinary price levels and insurance coverage. All clinical rates in this assessment are taken from the UK. All cost inputs are Polish.

Where a monetary figure is transferred from Germany, it is adjusted by two published factors: relative price level and relative insurance coverage.

The Swedish figure covers one diagnosis recorded through insurance claims. The UK figure covers all dogs presenting with any skin condition. The 74-fold difference is a result of definition, not of any difference between the two dog populations.

Sweden is included as a control rather than as a source of the rate applied. Its data quality is the highest available, but the quantity it measures is too narrow for market sizing.

05Results, and where the methods disagree

Comparability note

Methods 1 and 2 count vet visits and medicines together. Method 3 counts medicines only. They are not measuring the same thing, so the three results are not averaged or combined. An earlier version of this assessment treated them as equivalent; that error is corrected here.

The reported €70–150M is a judgement across methods 1 and 2. It is not a strict overlap of the two ranges, because these are scenario ranges rather than statistical confidence intervals.

For medicines the two methods do not agree. Calculated from Polish prices, medicines account for €30–80M. Taken from the German figure, they account for €110–188M — more than the estimate for vet visits and medicines combined. Both cannot be correct.

The German figure is the more likely source of the discrepancy. Vendor estimates for the same global market differ by a factor of two between publishers, and the German figure implies annual spend of €30–36 per dog on clinical dermatology drugs alone. However, the alternative explanation — that the Polish vet market figure understates spending on pets — cannot be ruled out from public data. A single measured Polish sales figure would resolve this.

Version history: v1 reported €70–150M from methods 1 and 2. v2 raised this to €90–160M on the basis that method 3 measured the same quantity. v3 reverts to €70–150M and treats method 3 as a medicines-only figure.

06Market structure

The supply side is published and requires no estimation. The National Chamber of Veterinarians maintains a complete public register of licensed veterinary establishments: approximately 7,600 facilities and 10,000 veterinarians. Most are single-practitioner practices. Distribution is concentrated in Mazovia (approximately 1,691 businesses), Greater Poland, Silesia and Lower Silesia. The register can be analysed by region and is suitable for sales territory planning and referral network mapping.

07Recommendations

Reporting

Say what the figure covers, every time. €70–150M covers vet visits and medicines; €30–80M covers medicines only, subject to the disagreement in section 05. A figure quoted without saying which it is cannot be compared with a market report or a competitor estimate.

Priority

Prioritise prevalence data over market-size reports. All methods are more sensitive to prevalence and spend per animal than to any market total. A Polish or regional prevalence study would improve the estimate more than an additional vendor report.

Internal

Check internal sales data before commissioning research. If the organisation sells in Poland, internal revenue combined with a market share estimate would settle the medicines disagreement at no cost.

External

Commission measured data only if the variance changes a decision. Kynetec VetTrak and PetTrak provide measured sales and patient-visit data through the veterinary channel by country. Cost is typically a five-figure custom quote. Confirm whether an existing licence covers Poland.

Caution

Treat single vendor figures as indicative only. Two established publishers report estimates differing by a factor of two for the same global market.

Definitions and calculations. Vet visits + medicines: treatment of canine skin disease at a veterinary practice, excluding retail and OTC preventatives. Medicines only: the drug component of that figure, taken as 40–55% of treatment cost. Wider market-report definition: adds antiparasitics, OTC topicals and supplements, and usually cats.
