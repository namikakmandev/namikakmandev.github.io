How big is dog dermatology in Poland? A number nobody publishes, built from public data.

Ask any market-research vendor for "the canine dermatology market in Poland" and you get a paywall, or a European figure with Poland buried inside it. No public source measures this directly. So the honest move is to build it — from data that is free and checkable: how many dogs Poland has, how often skin disease brings them to a vet, what a course of treatment costs, and how large the whole veterinary market is as a ceiling. Two independent methods, built to disagree, land in the same place: roughly €70–150 million a year. This page shows every step, every assumption, and exactly where a paid dataset would sharpen it.

Every euro figure on this page is modelled, not observed — there is no meter on this market. The badge marks where that is true. What is measured is the scaffolding: the dog count, the disease prevalence, the price points and the vet-market ceiling.

1. Why the number doesn't exist — and what does

"Dermatology market" is three different objects, and every source measures a different one: the drugs (Apoquel, Cytopoint, antiparasitics, topicals), the services (consultations, diagnostics, follow-ups), or both. Mixing them is the classic error here, so this study fixes one target up front: total owner spend on treating canine skin disease in Poland — drugs and services together — in a year.

What public data can supply are four solid anchors. None of them is the answer, but together they box it in:

The one genuinely borrowed number is prevalence. No Polish national dataset records how common skin disease is in dogs, so I use UK VetCompass primary-care data, where skin disorders are the second most common problem group at ~12.6% one-year prevalence (with otitis externa a further ~7.3%). Applying a UK rate to Polish dogs is the load-bearing assumption of the whole study — it travels with every figure below, and it is the first thing a paid dataset would replace.

2. Method one: bottom-up — from 8.1 million dogs

Multiply three things: how many dogs there are, the share that see a vet for a skin problem in a year, and what an owner spends on that episode. Each input is given a low / mid / high value so the uncertainty is visible rather than hidden in a single point.

The spend figure is a blend, not a chronic-case cost. Most skin episodes are a single consult plus a short topical course (~150–400 PLN); a minority are chronic atopic dogs on long-term Apoquel or Cytopoint with repeat visits and diagnostics (~1,500–4,000 PLN/yr). The blended average is pulled down by the many simple cases.

3. Method two: top-down — carving down the vet market

A completely different route, sharing none of the bottom-up inputs. Start from the whole Polish veterinary-services market and take successive slices: how much is companion-animal (not farm), how much of that is dogs, and how much of canine clinical revenue is dermatology.

Each slice is an assumed share, stated in the labels and in the data file. Dermatology is ~15–25% of consultation volume but a smaller share of revenue, because skin visits are cheaper on average than surgery or dentistry — so the derm slice is set at 10–20% of canine clinical revenue.

4. Where they meet — two methods, one band

The two methods share no inputs — one is built from dogs and diseases, the other from a market total and revenue shares. That makes their overlap meaningful rather than circular.

The bottom-up band is wide (€45–271M) because three uncertain inputs multiply. The top-down band (€25–118M) is tighter but leans low, since it can only capture spend that flows through veterinary clinics — not pet-shop antiparasitics or online Apoquel bought without a fresh consult. Their overlap, €70–150M with a point near €110M, is the honest headline.
Two methods that share no inputs agree on a band. That agreement is the finding — not the €110M point, which is a single draw from a distribution and should never be quoted alone.

5. Who delivers it — the supply side is fully public

One part of this market needs no modelling at all. The National Chamber of Veterinarians' register is a free, complete, public list of every licensed animal-treatment establishment in Poland — roughly 7,600 facilities and ~10,000 vets, most of them single-vet clinics, concentrated in Mazovia (Warsaw, ~1,691 businesses), Greater Poland, Silesia and Lower Silesia. You can count them by voivodeship and identify the derm-focused practices by hand. For anyone sizing a sales territory or a referral network, that register is the asset — no vendor required.

What to do with this

  1. Use it as a sizing skeleton, not a quote. The defensible statement is "canine dermatology in Poland is a €70–150M/yr market, modelled from public data" — never a single point. If you need a decision-grade number, method one tells you exactly which input to buy down: prevalence and spend-per-dog, in that order. What would change the estimate: a Polish (not UK) prevalence figure — it could move the whole band up or down by a third.
  2. If budget is small, buy one input. The Grand View Horizon Poland veterinary-dermatology-drug cut (~$1–5k) replaces the drug half of spend with a measured figure and instantly narrows the band. Cheapest path from estimate to evidence.
  3. If a real investment rides on it, buy Kynetec. Kynetec VetTrak / PetTrak (which absorbed Vetnosis) is the only source that measures Polish derm-drug sales and patient visits through the vet channel, monthly. That is where actual Apoquel/Cytopoint-class volumes for Poland live. Expect a five-figure custom quote — justified only when the number drives a market-entry or M&A decision.
  4. Grab the free supply-side asset now. The national vet register (section 5) costs nothing and is the highest-value public dataset here for anyone selling into Polish clinics. Pull it before commissioning anything paid.

What is measured vs modelled. Measured inputs: dog population — FEDIAF Facts & Figures 2025 (2023 data), 8.1M dogs. Price points — Polish vet-visit surveys (PETHELP, Vivigo, 2024–25: 100–250 PLN standard consult) and Apoquel 16mg pack listings (Allegro, 2026: ~46–90 PLN / 20 tablets). Vet-services market — IBISWorld Veterinary Services in Poland (~€1.4bn, 2026), Ken Research (~$0.9–1.5bn), Eurostat production value (€827.6M, 2023). Supply side — Krajowa Izba Lekarsko-Weterynaryjna / WetSystems national register. Everything expressed in euros is modelled — derived from these inputs, not observed.

The load-bearing proxy. No Polish dataset records canine skin-disease prevalence. This study borrows UK VetCompass primary-care prevalence (skin disorders ~12.6% one-year period prevalence, second most common disorder group; otitis externa ~7.3%) and general-practice caseload surveys (dermatology 8–40% of small-animal consultations; UK 21.4%, Montréal 18.8%). Applying a UK rate to Polish dogs is an assumption, not a fact, and it travels with every euro figure on this page. Breed mix, climate and treatment-seeking behaviour could all push the Polish rate off the UK one.

Definitions, so the numbers reproduce. Target = total annual owner spend on treating canine skin disease in Poland, drugs and services combined. Bottom-up = 8.1M × {8%, 12%, 18%} × {300, 500, 800} PLN. Top-down = €{0.9–1.4}bn × companion{50–65%} × dog{55–65%} × derm-revenue{10–20%}. EUR/PLN = 4.30 (2024–25 band 4.2–4.4), used only to express PLN results in euros. Reconciled band = the overlap of the two methods. No deflator is applied — all figures are a single recent year (2023–2026), not a time series. Known limits: the top-down route captures only clinic-channel spend and so leans low; the two methods are ranges, not point forecasts; "market size" figures from different vendors mix production value and revenue and are used only as an order-of-magnitude ceiling.
