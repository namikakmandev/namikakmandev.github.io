Broiler Margin Monitor

One ratio decides whether a chicken farm makes money: the price of chicken meat versus the price of feed. The Ministry publishes this "product/input parity" once a year. This monitor rebuilds it monthly, back to 2010, from official TÜİK producer price indices.

2020–22: the long squeeze The worst three margin years in the whole record (2.6–2.8). Feed inflation outran meat prices for three straight years — the stress every producer remembers, now visible in one line.
2023–24: repair Margins recovered to 3.48, then 3.82 — the best year since the series begins. Capacity grew into it.
2025–26: the supply response Late 2025 collapsed back to crisis levels (2.67 in December). Production then contracted — May 2026 output fell 16% — meat prices surged ~30% in four months, and the margin repaired itself to ≈3.3. A textbook hog-cycle, at broiler speed.
Method & honesty notes. Parity = TÜİK domestic producer price index for processed & preserved poultry meat (CPA C10.12) ÷ index for prepared farm-animal feeds (C10.91), scaled to TEPGE's official 2023 parity (3.48 kg feed per kg chicken). Both series: monthly, 2003=100, Jan 2010 – Jun 2026.

Prices are factory-gate, not farm-gate — for integrated producers the two move together, but levels are indicative. TÜİK collects no live-broiler farm-gate price (integration means the birds never trade on an open market), which is why this series has to be built rather than downloaded.
