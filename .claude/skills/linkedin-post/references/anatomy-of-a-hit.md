# Anatomy of a hit: "Anthropic vs SaaS"

Screenshot: `../assets/example-anthropic-vs-saas.png` (the LinkedIn feed,
16 October 2026). Author: Sergey (@SergeyCYW, SergeyCYW.substack.com).
Score when captured: **1,397 reactions, 102 comments, 41 reposts.** For scale,
this repository's own posts have run from a few dozen to a few hundred
reactions.

Nothing in it is clever production. It is a single PNG with two bar charts.
What it gets right is the choice of comparison and the discipline of leaving
everything else out.

## The claim

> Anthropic's $2T valuation exceeds 35 SaaS companies combined, despite
> generating 4.6× less revenue.

One sentence, two numbers, one multiplier. It is a comparison the reader
immediately wants to contest ("SaaS is ex-growth", "it's a private mark, not
a market cap", "revenue is annualising at 3× that"), and the contest is the
engagement. The comments are the post's second life.

## The peg

The text above the image: "Anthropic is preparing for an IPO. Its revenue
reached $4.73B in Q1 2026 and… more". The claim is tied to a news event of
the week (IPO preparation) and the first sentence carries a number. Nothing
else is visible before "more", and nothing else needs to be.

## The image, element by element

| Element | What it does |
|---|---|
| Headline "Anthropic vs SaaS" at roughly a fifth of the image height | Readable as a thumbnail; the post is legible in the notifications pane |
| Sub-headline in one sentence with the figures | The whole argument, for anyone who reads nothing else |
| Left panel: market cap, Anthropic $2.0T against a stack of 35 SaaS logos totalling $948B | The subject wins by 2× |
| Right panel: revenue Q2 2026, the same stack at $53.4B against Anthropic $11.5B | The subject loses by 4.6×, same companies, same colours, flipped |
| Logos as the stack segments (SAP, Salesforce, ServiceNow, Adobe, Intuit, Workday, Zoom, Veeva, Atlassian…), big to small | A "find yours" texture: readers zoom, tag, comment "we're in there"; it also makes 35 companies legible without 35 labels |
| Four numbers in total on the image | Nothing competes with the headline |
| A single accent colour (the Anthropic tan) against muted brand colours | The subject is the figure, the stack is the ground |
| No axes, no gridlines, no legend | Bars with their totals need none |
| Footer strip: X, Instagram, LinkedIn, Threads handle and the substack | Attribution survives every screenshot and repost |
| Aspect roughly 5:6, white ground | Fills the feed column on a phone; white reads as "chart", not "ad" |

## What is deliberately absent

- No methodology on the image. The caveats (private valuation, Q2 revenue
  versus an annualised rate, which 35 companies) live in the comments, where
  the author can answer them and each answer is a fresh impression.
- No link in the body. The substack is on the image strip and in the first
  comment.
- No hashtags visible in the first line.
- No video, no carousel. One PNG, which LinkedIn serves to everyone in full.

## What transfers to this repository's posts

The subjects are different (livestock, pharma, central banks, hyperscalers)
but the shape is the same:

1. One comparison that a sceptic will contest, stated with two numbers.
2. A peg to the week.
3. The headline and the finding on the image; the copy as a caption.
4. Five numbers or fewer on the image; the rest in the note and the first
   comment.
5. Recognisable names (companies, countries, institutions) as the texture.
6. Attribution strip and a source line on the image itself.
7. Single image, 4:5 or 1:1.

Applied to the tech-scalers post of October 2026, for instance: not a 63-second
video of four companies and eight bars, but one image headed "The AI build-out
stopped paying for itself", left panel the four companies' cash from
operations last quarter, right panel their capex, with the three negative
free cash flows in red along the bottom and Microsoft's in green, the four
logos as the bar labels, and "Alphabet's first negative quarter since its
2004 IPO" as the sub-headline. Four to five numbers. The video stays as a
second post a week later if the first one travels.
