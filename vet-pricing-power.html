Where the pricing power is

The parity study asked whether the price of a finished medicine tracks the ingredient inside it. This is the follow-up a seller actually needs: which parts of the book hold real price, which erode — and where the antibiotic line sits among them. Eight therapeutic classes, twenty-five years, all deflated, because a nominal price rising 2% a year in 3% inflation is a price cut.

Scope. United States only — no other statistical agency publishes prices at therapeutic-class level. These are BLS commodity indexes covering human and veterinary products together; there is still no veterinary-only index anywhere. One exception matters: WPU06380802, broad and medium spectrum antibiotics, is a real product line rather than a proxy — but it begins December 2009, eight years after the rest, so it is only ever compared on its own window.

Everything peaked, and then turned

Not one of the eight classes is at its real-terms high. Most peaked between 2017 and 2020.
This is the finding that outlives any single class. Every therapeutic class in the US pharmaceutical book peaked in real terms and has been falling since — most of them between January 2017 and May 2020. The aggregate index peaked May 2020 and has given back 17.2%. A pricing plan that assumes real price recovery is arguing against all eight series at once.

Before you quote any of this

1 · These are index numbers, not prices. Nothing here is a dollar, a margin or a multiple. Every figure is a change between two dated observations — the only thing that survives a change of base. The antibiotic series sits on a Dec 2009 base and the eight classes on Jun 2001, which is exactly why they are never compared over each other's spans.
2 · Human and veterinary are mixed. BLS publishes no veterinary-only line. Broad-spectrum antibiotics are used in both human and animal medicine, and this index cannot separate them. If the animal-health price path diverged from the human one, this series would not show it — that gap is the open question, not something to paper over.
3 · Prices only, no volumes. A class can hold real price while its units collapse. Nothing here says how much was sold, so no revenue conclusion follows from a price conclusion.
What would settle it: internal net-price and volume history for the same classes, laid against these curves. If your own antibiotic realisation tracked the +12% line, the erosion is the market. If it tracked the +29% book, it is not, and the difference is worth knowing before the next price round.

So what

Three instructions, each with the thing that would change it.
The spread · now
Stop reading the spread as pricing power.
Price +12.0%, ingredient −29.3%, so the gross spread widened about 58% — but the book gained 29.4% and the parent class 26.5% on the identical window. The margin came from input deflation, and input deflation is not a durable asset: it stops the moment ingredient prices turn.
Would change if: the antibiotic index outpaces WPU0638 over any rolling five years, or the ingredient index rises for eight consecutive quarters while the antibiotic price holds.
The trend · 2027 planning
Plan for continued real erosion, not recovery.
Antibiotics peaked January 2019 and has lost 27.6% in real terms since — faster than its parent class at −18.6% and far faster than CNS at −9.2%. All eight classes are below their peaks. Nothing in twenty-five years of this data supports a recovery assumption.
Would change if: the antibiotic index posts four consecutive quarters of real increase, or the aggregate book turns up first — it has led the class turns before.
The portfolio · if expansion is on the table
The real price sits in cardiovascular and CNS.
+101.2% and +55.7% on the same window against your +12.0%. Treat this as a map of where pricing power exists in pharmaceutical manufacturing generally — with the caveat that these classes are human-dominated, so read it as a direction to investigate, not a product plan.
Would change if: a veterinary-specific price series ever becomes available and shows a different class ordering. Until then this ranking is about pharma, not about animal health specifically.

Deflator
US CPI-U, FRED CPIAUCSL. The CPI has no observation for October 2025; that month is dropped from every deflated series.
Completeness & limits WPU063801–08 is the complete class set: WPU063809, 063810 and 063811 were probed and do not exist, so no class is silently dropped.
Breaks
The largest single-month move differs by class and by month — respiratory −15.1% Jan 2025, cardiovascular +13.8% Oct 2017, antibiotics +12.8% Jul 2014. A survey redesign would move every series in the same month; these do not, so they are read as market events.
What is missing
Any veterinary-only price index, anywhere. Volumes. Net realised price after rebates — these are producer list-basis indexes, and on branded US drugs the gross-to-net gap is large and has itself grown over time, so real net realisation likely rose more slowly than these curves. Class rankings compare list-basis series against each other, which is fairer than quoting their levels — but the caveat stands.
