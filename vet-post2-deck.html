METHOD

Straight from the filings. Nothing modelled.

Source
Annual 10-K figures via SEC EDGAR's XBRL API (SEC: US Securities and Exchange Commission; the 10-K is the audited annual report every US-listed company must file) — machine-readable, public, keyless.
Measure
COGS, R&D and SG&A as % of revenue — mean of FY2023–25 per company, median across each group so no single filer drags the figure.
Two clean groups
Human: Pfizer, Lilly, AbbVie, Bristol Myers Squibb. Animal: Zoetis, Elanco, Phibro — pure-play filers only. Merck (~9% animal health) is shown but pooled with neither group. Animal-health bars are shown unattributed: the finding is the group contrast, not any one filer's margin. All figures re-derivable from the filings above.
What COGS is not
COGS is not the ingredient. No filer splits API out of its cost of goods — the ingredient from Part 1 sits inside these bars, unquantified.

WHY IT MATTERS

Two cost structures, two price histories.

R&D-built P&L · human
+75%
Real change in the US producer (factory-gate) price of human-dominated pharma over the clean 20-year window (2001–2021). R&D is financed through price — current revenue funds future products.
Manufacturing-built P&L · veterinary
−4%
Real change in the US veterinary producer price on the same window (BLS WPU0634). Manufacturing follows its input costs — and those inputs became cheaper. Factory-gate, not what clinics or owners pay.
For anyone applying human-pharma benchmarks to animal health: the benchmark does not transfer. Veterinary prices have followed a manufacturing cost base for forty years. Both figures come from public data and both hold after inflation adjustment.

SCOPE & LIMITS

Scope and limitations.

Small samples, stated
Four human filers, three animal. Medians, not means; three fiscal years averaged. This is an anatomy sketch, not a census.
Global, not US-only
10-K figures are worldwide consolidated. Nothing here is a US-market cost share.
Heterogeneity is real
Inside animal health, COGS runs 29% to 69% across the three filers — petcare and nutrition are different businesses sharing a label.
Accounting seams
Filers switched revenue/R&D tags mid-history (unioned, logged); acquired-IPR&D inflates some R&D lines (Merck). No headcount tag exists in XBRL — employee costs stay unmeasured.

CLOSE · SOURCES

Same ingredient market, different businesses.

Why human R&D is high
The pipeline race. Exclusivity expires after roughly a decade, so the next molecule must always be in trials — and human trials are long, costly and mostly fail. A fifth of revenue goes back in.
Why animal COGS is high
Price near cost, and scale. Prices have tracked inflation for four decades, so cost is a large share of price. Within animal health the share also falls with size — 69% at the smallest filer, 29% at the largest.
The conclusion
Human pharma sells innovation — priced ahead of cost to fund what's next. Animal health sells production — priced near cost, earning through volume and efficiency. So: benchmark within the segment.
