Pet Data · Dog Years, Properly

Your dog is older than you think.
And the “multiply by 7” rule is simply wrong.

Scientists mapped chemical marks on dog DNA against human DNA and produced a real conversion. It is not a straight line — dogs sprint through youth, then age slowly.

31
human-equivalent years of a 1-year-old dog — not 7
62
a 7-year-old dog is already middle-aged, heading into senior care
×7
the old rule: too young early, too old late — wrong at both ends
Epigenetic formula (Wang et al., 2020) The “×7” myth
This is not trivia — it is a care calendar. A dog covers three human decades in its first year, which is why early-life vaccination, nutrition and neutering decisions matter so much. And a 7-year-old dog at ~62 human years is why senior screening should start years before most owners think to ask for it.
Source & method · Wang, T. et al., “Quantitative Translation of Dog-to-Human Aging by Conserved Remodeling of the DNA Methylome”, Cell Systems 11(2), 2020 (UC San Diego School of Medicine). Formula: human-equivalent age = 16 × ln(dog age in years) + 31. Built from DNA-methylation profiles of 104 Labrador Retrievers (4 weeks–16 years) aligned to 320 humans (1–103 years). Limits: derived from a single breed, so it does not capture breed-size effects — large breeds age faster and live shorter lives than small ones; the curve is undefined below ~1 month. The “×7” rule has no scientific basis; it is a rule of thumb of unknown origin.
