Group & Category Check

Averages lie by omission. A mean can be moved by two invoices; a t-test can be significant and meaningless at the same time; a χ² can be p < 0.001 for an association too weak to act on. Paste your data and this checker runs the comparison with honest defaults: Welch's t-test (no equal-variance assumption), effect sizes with confidence intervals on every result, rank-based and label-shuffle alternatives beside every formula, and outlier, skew and expected-count checks that run whether or not you ask. Everything runs in your browser — no data leaves the page.

How this checker works — honest defaults, explained
  1. Welch, not Student. The classic t-test assumes both groups have equal spread; real groups rarely do. Welch's version drops the assumption and costs nothing when it happens to hold, so it is the default here, not an option three menus deep.
  2. Effect size is not optional. A p-value says a difference is detectable; it says nothing about whether it is big enough to matter. Every comparison reports Hedges' g (bias-corrected), ω², or bias-corrected Cramér's V, each with plain-language calibration. A tiny p with a tiny effect means "definitely real, definitely irrelevant" — common at large n, and worth saying out loud.
  3. Three routes to every answer. Each comparison runs the classical formula, a rank-based test that ignores outliers and shape, and a label-shuffle permutation test that assumes nothing at all. When they agree, the verdict is safe. When they disagree, the disagreement is diagnostic — usually outliers or skew — and the checker says which line to trust.
  4. Assumption checks run themselves. Outliers by the MAD rule (robust, so the outliers cannot hide the threshold), skew and kurtosis, Jarque–Bera normality, variance ratios, and expected cell counts in crosstabs. In most software these are optional extras; here they print before the tests do.
  5. A significant ANOVA names no culprit. "Not all groups are equal" is all F can say. Comparing every pair afterwards is k(k−1)/2 tests, and the checker prints the Bonferroni bar those comparisons must clear.
  6. The ledger. Every check you run this session raises the honest significance bar, and the checker shows it. Slicing data until something turns significant is the oldest trick in the book; here the book keeps count.

Method · Welch's t with Welch–Satterthwaite df; Hedges' g (small-sample corrected) with CI; Mann–Whitney with tie-corrected normal approximation and probability of superiority; label-shuffle permutation tests with fixed seeds; one-way ANOVA with ω²; Kruskal–Wallis with tie correction; χ² with bias-corrected Cramér's V and expected-count checks; MAD outlier rule at 3.5; Jarque–Bera normality. p-values via exact t, F (incomplete beta) and χ² (incomplete gamma) distributions, validated against textbook critical values. Companion tool for paired series: Correlation & Regression Check.
