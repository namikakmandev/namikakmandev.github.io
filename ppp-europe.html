How expensive is each European country?

Every number is a price level index: what a common basket costs in a country, relative to the EU-27 average (= 100), at market exchange rates. Computed from Eurostat's purchasing power parities, so levels are comparable across countries — orange is dearer than the EU average, blue is cheaper. Price levels only: this says nothing about margins or markups anywhere in any supply chain.

Source: Eurostat, purchasing power parities and price level indices (prc_ppp_ind), indicator PLI_EU27_2020; annual; fetched via this repo's Actions pipeline (). GDP and the two consumption aggregates run from 1995; category detail from 2003 (hospital services 2006). Values for earlier years at category level are Eurostat back-estimates around the COICOP 2018 revision — treat long category series with care. Candidate-country figures (TR, RS, MK, ME, AL, BA) follow Eurostat's published series; Kosovo sits in the dataset's country dimension but publishes no price level values. The data file also carries the US and Japan for context; this page keeps to Europe. A price level index compares a whole basket — it is not a cost-of-living ranking for any single household, and it measures no distribution margin: markups are a separate assessment with a separate evidence base.
