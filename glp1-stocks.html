The GLP-1 trade. One molecule class, two very different stocks.

GLP-1 receptor agonists — semaglutide, tirzepatide and the rest — became the defining drug story of the decade, treating diabetes and then obesity. Two companies own the franchise: Eli Lilly and Novo Nordisk. They rode the same science, yet the market has priced them apart to a degree that is hard to believe from the headlines alone. This page pulls monthly total-return prices (split- and dividend-adjusted) for twelve listed GLP-1 names and two benchmarks, and lets the tape tell the story. All figures update automatically each month from the source data.

Method & caveats. Prices are monthly adjusted closes from Yahoo Finance, in USD, fetched through this site's GitHub Actions pipeline (scripts/fetch.py → data/glp1-stocks.json) and recomputed in your browser. "Adjusted" means split- and dividend-adjusted, so the lines are total return, not price alone; the S&P 500 line is the raw index level (no dividends), so it slightly understates the market against the dividend-paying stocks — the biotech ETF (XBI) is the fairer sector benchmark.

Non-US companies trade here as ADRs (Novo Nordisk, AstraZeneca, Roche, Zealand), so their USD returns embed currency moves; Zealand's US line is thin OTC while its primary listing is Copenhagen. Comparability: Lilly and Novo are GLP-1-driven stories, but Amgen, AstraZeneca, Roche and Pfizer are diversified — GLP-1 is one program among many, so read those as context, not as "GLP-1 returns". Survivorship: Metsera (a pure-play obesity biotech) is absent because Pfizer acquired and delisted it in November 2025; Boehringer Ingelheim (survodutide) is private. Indexed charts rebase each series to 100 at the base month; drawdown is the percent decline from each stock's own running all-time high within the window. Not investment advice.
