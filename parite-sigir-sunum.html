WHAT YOU ARE LOOKING AT

One ratio: what a kilo of meat buys in feed.

THE MEASURE
PARITY =
meat price ÷ feed cost
Higher = meat outpacing feed → the producer earns more.
Lower = feed eating the margin.
THE SCALE
Each market is indexed to its own 2016 average = 100.
So 184 means US producers earn 84% more per kilo of feed than they did in 2016. The three lines are comparable in shape, not in level.

APPENDIX · HOW THE NUMBERS ARE MADE

One month, start to finish.

United States, June 2026 — every step, from the published price to the profit line.
1
Take two published prices. US producer price index, June 2026 — slaughter cattle 398.12, corn 163.14. Both straight from BLS, unadjusted.
2
Divide them → parity. 398.12 ÷ 163.14 = 2.44 — one unit of cattle price buys 2.44 units of corn price.
3
Index to 2016 → comparable across countries. The 2016 average parity was 1.32. So 2.44 ÷ 1.32 × 100 = 184. The same step is done for the EU and Türkiye with their own 2016 averages.
4
Count the plateau. Take the twelve-month average. Count the run of months it stays within 10% of its own peak. Currently 13; the five previous runs were 10 to 23.
5
Turn it into a P&L. Revenue 100. Calf 45 and other costs 15 held fixed. Feed = 35 × (long-run mean parity 1.08 ÷ this month’s 2.44) = 15. Profit = 100 − 45 − 15 − 15 = +25.
STEPS 1–4 ARE ARITHMETIC ON PUBLISHED DATA · STEP 5 IS THE ONLY MODELLED PART
