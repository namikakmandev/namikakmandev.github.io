Türkiye built the renewables. The power price followed the gas market.

Renewables' share of Turkish electricity went from a sixth in 2008 to over two fifths in 2025, and solar alone now makes more power than the whole wind and solar fleet did in 2020. The price an industrial user pays, in lira of 2025, is no lower than it was in 2008. It moved with the European gas price and the lira, not with the share.

Every figure below is recomputed from the committed data files by scripts/tr_power_study.py. Money is in lira at 2025 prices (TÜİK CPI) or in euro at the half-year rate; the deflator and the breaks are listed at the end.

Where Türkiye's electricity comes fromGeneration by source, TWh a year, 2000 to 2025. Ember via Our World in Data.

Gas was the largest source in 2010, close to half of generation. By 2025 it made a fifth. What replaced it was not only renewables: coal generation more than doubled, and coal is now the biggest single source. Wind and solar went from nothing to a fifth. Hydro is the swing: a dry 2025 took it to its lowest share of the period, and gas filled the gap.

Generation, not consumption; Türkiye is a small net importer. 2025 is Ember's provisional year. Source: electricity-mix (Ember Yearly Electricity Data via Our World in Data), TR|coal … TR|solar, TWh.

The share rose. The real price did not fall.Renewables' share of generation against the industrial power price in lira of 2025, 2008 to 2025.

The industrial price (Eurostat's band for a mid-size user, before VAT) was flat in real terms for a decade, about three lira of 2025 per kilowatt-hour. It rose from 2019, spiked in the second half of 2022 to more than three times its 2010 level, and fell back to four lira by 2025. Across the same years the renewables share climbed from 17% to 43%. If the build-out lowered the price, this series does not show it: the correlation between the two is positive, and once the sample is discounted for how slowly both series move it is not distinguishable from zero.

What the price did followThe Turkish real industrial price and the European natural-gas price, annual, 2008 to 2025.

Set the price against the European gas price and the shape matches: the 2019 climb, the 2022 spike, the fall after. Türkiye imports almost all of its gas and much of its coal, and the plants that set the marginal price burn them. The level correlation is strong and survives the autocorrelation discount; year-to-year changes are noisier, which is what a regulated tariff that lags the market looks like.

Households saw something else entirelyHousehold price in euro, all taxes, against the industrial price in euro, 2008 to 2025.

The household tariff, in euro, is below where it was in 2008 and less than half its 2013 level. That is not the renewables either: it is a regulated tariff that was held while the lira fell, which is a policy choice and a subsidy, and it barely moved in 2022 while the market-facing industrial band more than doubled. Read the household line as policy, not as a market price.

What to do with this

Not supportedAn industrial buyer in Türkiye
Budget 2026 power on the lira and the imported gas price, not on the renewables share.
What would change it: a wholesale price series (EPİAŞ day-ahead) parting from the gas price for two consecutive years. It is not in this collection yet.
SupportedAn investor in Turkish generation
Solar is the growth source: it added more TWh in 2025 than any other. Coal is the base; gas is the swing when hydro fails, as in 2025.
What would change it: two wet years in a row, or a carbon price on coal. Watch hydro's share, printed above, each spring.
Read as policyAnyone quoting household prices
Do not compare Turkish household tariffs with European ones as market prices; they are administered and lag the lira.
What would change it: a tariff reform that indexes households to cost, which would show as the household line converging on the industrial one.

Read with care

  • Deflator. Lira prices are divided by TÜİK's consumer price index, 2025 = 100 (dataset tr-cpi-ppi, series cpi, half-year means). Euro prices use the half-year mean EUR/TRY (tr-fx-monthly, eur_try). A different deflator, the domestic PPI, would flatten the 2022 spike less, since producer prices rose faster than consumer prices that year.
  • What the price is. Eurostat's band IC is a mid-size industrial consumer (500 to 1 999 MWh a year) excluding VAT and other recoverable taxes, in national currency. It is a published tariff band, not the wholesale market price, and Turkish tariffs are set by the regulator in steps: half-yearly points can lag the market by a period.
  • The test. Eighteen annual points of two slow-moving series. The effective sample after the lag-1 autocorrelation discount is about six for the share and thirteen for the gas price; p_adj is read at that size. A correlation is not a cause either way: the gas result says the price moved with the fuel, not that renewables had no effect on what the price would otherwise have been. That counterfactual needs a market-price series and a merit-order model, neither of which is here.
  • Breaks. None known in the price series; the 2022 values are real, not a break. Ember's 2025 is provisional. The renewables share counts hydro, so it falls in dry years without any plant closing.
  • Not measured. Wholesale prices, installed capacity, generation cost by source, subsidies to the household tariff. The household conclusion rests on the tariff moving against the market band, not on a subsidy figure.
  • Reproduce it. python3 scripts/tr_power_study.py rewrites data/tr-power-study.json from data/electricity-mix.json, data/renewables-share.json, data/elec-eu.json, data/energy-household-eu.json, data/tr-cpi-ppi.json, data/tr-fx-monthly.json and data/wb-commodities.json.
